# Comparable games and actual player substitutes

Inspired by the structured comparison approaches of the MIT-licensed `Abaudat/steam-market-research-skill` and `AlterLab-IEU/AlterLab_GameForge`. This document adapts their **method**, not revenue formulas or fixed success-rate claims. See `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` for attribution.

## Select a decision-relevant comparison set

Do not default to Steam or to one country. Choose the platforms and markets the intended player actually uses, including Chinese-language Steam, TapTap, mini-games, app stores or other authorized sources where appropriate.

- Direct: same player job/genre and comparable scope, distribution model, price and production quality.
- Indirect: different genre or even non-game activity competing for the same time, emotion or social setting.
- Aspirational: an exceptional benchmark that calibrates quality but is **not** a typical financial outcome.
- Unsuccessful or ordinary peers: include if observable and comparable to counter survivorship bias; poor storefront visibility is not proof of weak design.

Choose only enough examples to decide what to test next. Distinguish `observed` availability, reviews, date and price from estimates. Label commercial figures as unknown when no reliable underlying data exists. Never turn review count into a universal sales multiplier or extrapolate US/Steam sales to Chinese distribution without validation.

Always ask **why a player would choose a new product instead of an existing alternative**, and what the older product already does well. A popular genre proves attention to current supply, not a new entrant's reachable demand.

## Copy-speed pressure

Imagine a comparable product with the same main mechanic appearing soon. Identify the player's remaining reason to choose this product: execution/feel, finished content, creator/community relationship, trusted quality, delivery/access, price, or no durable reason. For a short-lived novelty game, fast time to market and low-cost learning can be rational; do not impose a permanent competitive moat as a gate. Treat pressure tests as scenarios, not forecasts or AI-generated win probabilities.

Output the **one observable discriminator** that could change the development choice; then select a lowest-cost evidence method using `validation.md`.
