# Player choice under abundant game supply

Do not award an opaque 'market potential' number. Match the test to the actual choice the player makes, in the real distribution context, against a credible alternative.

## Three separate questions

1. **Attention / 被看见**: Can a target player discover, recognize and understand the proposition? Useful observations include impressions with known denominator, comprehensibility, and platform/creative source. Clicks or likes do not prove actual play.
2. **Choice / 被选择**: Does someone voluntarily pick this game over available substitutes? Observe a choice at a real invitation, demo, install/start, wishlist or paid purchase opportunity. State recruitment and friction; a survey intention is not an actual choice.
3. **Experience outcome / 兑现体验**: Does the player obtain the value promised? For replayable games, assess voluntary return, invited friends or continued activity. For a finite narrative or paid single-session title, completion, recommendation, satisfaction and purchase may be more appropriate than DAU or D7. Never require all games to maximize retention.

These phases are diagnostic, not mandatory funding gates or causal attribution claims. Avoid arbitrary thresholds; agree on test conditions, alternative explanation and plausible business consequence **before** observing outcomes.

## Rapid imitation scenario

Ask: 'If a competitor reproduced the mechanic and comparable presentation quickly, what player-observable reason remains to choose or recommend this version?' Explore craft/feel, specific content, community, discoverability, service trust, distribution relationships, or an explicitly time-bounded novelty window. If no advantage is proven, identify the weakest assumption and a cheap comparison test; do not invent moat scores or force long-term defensibility on every project.

## Avoid false proof

- Creative impressions -> awareness only, subject to source/placement bias.
- Signups or wishlists -> expressed intent, not revenue.
- Short recruited tests -> experience in the tested sample, not market retention.
- Real repeat behavior -> descriptive evidence, not necessarily incremental value over competitors without an appropriate comparison.
- Steam reviews or shop ranking -> selected user feedback and relative visibility, not representative consumer demand.

If the only support is AI taste judgment, present a `HYPOTHESIS` and propose a human/player test rather than a positive verdict.
